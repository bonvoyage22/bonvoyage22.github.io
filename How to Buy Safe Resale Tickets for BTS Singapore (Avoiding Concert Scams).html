3 Major Scams to avoid on Social Media

If you are tempted to buy from individuals on X (Twitter), Carousell, or Telegram, look out for these massive red flags:

  • • The "Proof of Purchase" Video: Scammers can edit screen recordings of their Ticketmaster accounts. Never trust a video or screenshot as definitive proof of ticket ownership.
  • • Deposit Requests: Anyone asking you to "pay a 50% deposit to hold the ticket" may disappear once the money clears. Avoid sending deposits to unverified sellers.
  • • Direct Bank Transfers (PayNow/PayLah): These payment methods may offer limited or no buyer protection for person-to-person ticket transactions. Once the money is transferred, recovering it can be difficult.

Tips for Buying Your Resale Tickets Securely

When navigating secondary marketplaces, follow these 3 rules to maximize your safety:

  1. • Check the Delivery Method: For Singapore, look for listings specified as Electronic Delivery or Digital Transfer. Where supported, these methods transfer tickets directly into your ticket account. However, confirm the transfer process with the official ticketing provider, as a digital ticket does not automatically eliminate every resale risk.
  2. • Factor in the Fees: Ticket platforms charge service fees at checkout. Review the complete final price before paying, including all applicable fees and taxes. Keep this in mind when budgeting for your floor or tiered seats.
  3. • Buy in Pairs Early: If you are traveling to Singapore with a friend, try to buy adjacent seats together. Split single listings are much harder to match up later, closer to the event dates in December.

Don't risk your money — and your chance to see BTS — on unverified social media deals. Stick to established platforms, review their buyer protection policies, and verify ticket transfer eligibility before completing your purchase.
