    • ❤️ Major Scams to avoid on Social Media

    1. The "Proof of Purchase" Video: Scammers on X (Twitter), Carousell, or Telegram can edit screen recordings of their Ticketmaster accounts. Never trust a video or screenshot as definitive proof of ticket ownership.
    2. Deposit Requests: Anyone asking you to "pay a 50% deposit to hold the ticket" may disappear once the money clears. Avoid sending deposits to unverified sellers.
    3. Direct Bank Transfers (PayNow/PayLah): These payment methods may offer limited or no buyer protection for person-to-person ticket transactions. Once the money is transferred, recovering it can be difficult.

    💜 Tips for buying BTS Tickets Singapore Safely

    1. Check the Delivery Method: For Singapore, look for listings specified as Electronic Delivery or Digital Transfer. Where supported, these methods transfer tickets directly into your ticket account. However, confirm the transfer process with the official ticketing provider, as a digital ticket does not automatically eliminate every resale risk.
    2. Factor in the Fees: Ticket platforms charge service fees at checkout. Review the complete final price before paying, including all applicable fees and taxes. Keep this in mind when budgeting for your floor or tiered seats.
    3. Buy in Pairs Early: If you are traveling to Singapore with someone, try to buy adjacent seats together. Split single listings are much harder to match up later, closer to the event dates in December.
    4. How to Check for Electronic Delivery or Digital Transfer: Sellers select a delivery type when creating their listing
      • Filter by Delivery Type: Open the Filters menu on the event page and look for Mobile Transfer, Electronic, or Instant Download (if available).
      • Check the Listing Card Before Checkout: Tap into a specific seat listing and look under the section/row details for tags like "Mobile Transfer" (meaning the seller intends to push the ticket via an official ticketing app) rather than "Paper" or "Meet-up."
      • Review the Expected Delivery Date: On the checkout screen, check the "In-hand / Ready by" date. For Singapore shows (December 17, 19, 20, and 22, 2026), Ticketmaster.sg releases mobile-only tickets roughly two weeks before each show (early December).
      • How Resellers Bypass It: Because official account-to-account transfer is restricted, sellers on secondary platforms often attempt to fulfill "Electronic Delivery" orders by handing over their entire Ticketmaster.sg account login credentials or sending a screenshot/browser link which leaves buyers vulnerable to the seller logging back in or selling the same barcode multiple times.
      • Unsold production holds or cancelled/voided scalper tickets occasionally reappear at face value on Ticketmaster Singapore closer to the December show dates.
